1. Read this first: how a professional thinks about this board

I have made my living betting football for a long time, and I want to tell you the thing nobody tells beginners: the model is not the product. The price is the product.

Sportsbooks post a line, say Buffalo −3.5. That line is the market's best guess at a number that makes both sides equally likely, and the books charge you a fee on every bet (the "vig") whichever side you take. If you bet at random you lose the fee, slowly, forever. To win, you need one of two things: either you know the true probability better than the market does (an edge from information), or you find a book charging a better price than the market's true number (an edge from shopping). The second is far more common, far more reliable, and it is where almost all of my money has come from.

This board was built by someone who understood that. It has a model that projects every game, and the model is respectable: over 3,416 NFL games since 2014 it misses the final margin by 10.02 points on average, against 9.91 for the closing line. Close, but not better. "About as accurate as the market" does not pay the vig, and the board does not pretend otherwise. It measures how much of the model's claimed edge has ever turned into money and sizes the bets from that. On the NFL board the answer is almost none: over 2,239 graded bets from 2018 to 2025 the model claimed +4.8% and returned −1.9%. So the NFL model's own opinion stays off the bet card. In college the same measurement said about half, but when the college model was regraded against Pinnacle's real 2024–2025 lines its edge did not hold up, so since November 1 the college model's own bets carry no stake either.

So where is the money?

Three places, and the board is built around them in this order:

  1. Prices that beat Pinnacle. Pinnacle is the sharpest book in the world: it takes big bets from professionals and moves its line fast, so its price with the vig removed is the best estimate of the true chance there is. When DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM or Kalshi pays more than that true chance is worth, you have an edge with no model at all. These go stale within hours.
  2. The opener. When the model disagrees with the opening line by 2 or more points (NFL spreads), the line has historically moved toward the model before kickoff, and the side has won 55.4% at the opening number (1,012 bets, +5.6% per dollar, ±3.0%; since 2019, 54.7% and +4.3% ± 3.7% on 646). Read that sentence carefully: the edge is the line moving toward the model. The same sides, graded at the closing line after the move, win 51.9%, below the 52.4% you need at −110. Once the market has moved, the edge is gone. That is why the board has a whole section on line movement and why your phone buzzes when a gap appears.
  3. Wind unders (NFL). Outdoor games with 10 to 19 mph of wind have gone under the closing total 56.8% of the time since 2014 (498-379-9), and 59.2% since 2019. The total falls as the forecast firms up, so these are bet early, not late. Until there is a season of these graded on forecasts rather than recorded wind, they are staked at half size.

Everything else on the page, including the model's own leans, is information. Useful, honest information, but not a bet.

The one sentence to remember Bet prices that beat the sharp line, bet the model's disagreements with the opener before the market moves, and when the market has already moved, either tease the line or pass. The board now tells you which of those three situations every bet is in.

What a professional does not do with this board

  • Bet the biggest EV number on the page. EV is claimed by the model; the haircut says how much of it has ever been real. On November 11 the college board's biggest number was +11.0% on ORST +13.5, on the model alone, with no stake; record-adjusted it reads 0.0%. The stake column, not the EV column, says what the board would bet.
  • Chase an opener bet after the line moved. If the board says Missed, the edge you read about in the record is not on offer any more.
  • Play leans. The Model leans document exists so that you can see what the model thinks without confusing it with a bet.
  • Judge the board on a week. Fifty bets is noise. Closing line value (section 14) is the short-run signal; the win-loss record takes seasons.

2. The arithmetic: odds, vig, probability, EV, Kelly, teasers

Everything on these boards is built from a handful of formulas. You do not need to compute them, but you need to know what they mean.

American odds and implied probability

−110Risk 110 to win 100. Implied chance = 110 / (110 + 100) = 52.4%. This is the break-even for a standard spread bet.
+150Risk 100 to win 150. Implied chance = 100 / (150 + 100) = 40.0%.
−200Risk 200 to win 100. Implied chance = 66.7%.

The vig, and "no-vig"

A book quotes both sides of a spread at −110. Each implies 52.4%, together 104.8%. The extra 4.8% is the book's margin. Remove it and you have the no-vig (fair) chance: 50% each. When the board says "Pinnacle's no-vig number" it means Pinnacle's two prices with the margin taken out, which is the market's honest estimate of each side's chance.

Expected value (EV)

EV per dollar = chance of winning × profit if you win − chance of losing × stake. At −110 with a true 55% chance: 0.55 × 0.909 − 0.45 × 1 = +5.0%. The board shows EV two ways:

  • EV vs sharp (the price tier): the price against Pinnacle's no-vig chance. No model involved. This is the most trustworthy number on the site.
  • EV vs model: the price against the market-adjusted projection's chance. Read it together with the haircut.

Kelly staking, and the caps

Kelly is the stake that grows a bankroll fastest for a given edge: edge divided by the net odds. Full Kelly is far too aggressive when your edge estimate is uncertain, so the board uses quarter Kelly, and caps it three ways:

Per bet3% of bankroll.
Per game5%, all sides together. If a game's bets add to more, every stake in that game shrinks by the same factor.
Per week10%, everything on the sheet that week together, scaled the same way. On November 11 the NFL Week 10 sheet came to 3.7%, under the cap; next week's early lines (Week 11) came to 8.7%, also under it. With its model edges unstaked, the college Week 11 sheet came to 5.1%.

Stakes are shown in dollars at the bankroll in the box at the top of the Games page (default $1,000) and as a percentage. Change the box to your bankroll and every stake on the site follows.

The haircut

The model's chance is not used as is. Each Tuesday (NFL) and Monday (college) the full backtest measures the share of the model's claimed edge that turned into realized profit over the last eight seasons. The stake is sized from the model's chance shrunk toward the market's by that share. NFL: 11% on November 11 (8% a week earlier and 0% the week before; the measurement is noisy, ±29%), and the model's own NFL edges stay off the card either way. College: measured at 52%, but capped at 0 NEW since November 1, because the college model's edge against consensus lines did not hold against Pinnacle's real 2024–2025 numbers. The college sheet says so in its note. If the measurement is missing, the board sizes nothing rather than everything (it "fails closed").

Bucket stakes

Opener and wind bets do not need the model to be right; they rest on their bucket's own record. When the model sizes nothing for one of them, the board sizes a bucket stake: quarter Kelly on Pinnacle's chance for that side at the book's own line and price, plus the bucket's edge over a fair line (its win rate shrunk halfway to break-even, less 50%). Two details matter:

  • The book is the one with the best price against Pinnacle, not the one the model likes best. The bucket's win rate was measured at roughly −110 at the opener; it does not transfer to a plus-money price on a worse number.
  • Once the line has moved toward the side, the rate used is the bucket's win rate at the close (51.9% for NFL spreads 2+), which is below break-even and sizes nothing.
  • Wind unders are staked at half size NEW until they have a season graded on forecasts. Their record was built on the wind actually measured at the game, and live bets are placed on a forecast.

The closing-line-value guard NEW

Every kind of bet (price, opener, wind, model) is watched against the closing line. Once a kind has 50 or more graded bets and its average closing line value is below zero by more than two standard errors, it carries no stake until it recovers. Its bets stay on the sheet, marked, and stay in the ledger, so the record keeps measuring them. The Bet sheet's note names any kind that is muted. On November 11 no NFL kind had 50 graded bets yet (price bets were at 26), and the college price tier, with 92, was at −0.1% ± 1.1% and so not muted; the Closing line value table on the Model record page shows how close each one is (section 14).

Teasers

A 6-point teaser moves the line 6 points your way on every leg, and every leg must win. A two-leg teaser at −120 breaks even when each leg wins √(120/220) = 73.9%. The board prices each leg's chance off the market's own key-number curve (no model) and marks those that clear. Wong legs are NFL sides that a 6-point tease carries across both 3 and 7: favorites of −7.5 to −8.5 (teased to −1.5 to −2.5) and underdogs of +1.5 to +2.5 (teased to +7.5 to +8.5). They are the classic teaser legs; price them, do not bet them by rule.

Kalshi

Kalshi is an exchange. Its prices are contract prices in cents, and it charges a fee: about 7% × p × (1−p) per contract to take a price, 1.75% to rest an order. The board converts every Kalshi price to an effective American price net of the fee ("net") before any EV. Where a resting order would be worth posting, the board says "Post on Kalshi at X¢": the highest price at which a maker order is still +EV. That price is now computed against the chance the stakes are sized from, which on the NFL board is Pinnacle's own number. A resting order may never fill.

What is on the sheet versus what is in the ledger

Every bet that appears on the sheet is written into the ledger the first time it appears, at that price, and never changed. If the board later changes its mind (a quarterback ruled out, a line that moved to the model), the bet comes off the sheet and the ledger marks it withdrawn. By November 11 the NFL ledger had 119 bets: 29 kept on the sheet (to kickoff, or still on it this week) and 90 withdrawn before kickoff, most of them by the "Missed" rule. How fresh it is

The board's build writes the section, and then every line snapshot re-measures it at Pinnacle's newest line, without a rebuild. The stamp under the heading says which line it used. Snapshots run three times a day, hourly from Sunday evening to Tuesday night (when new lines open), hourly on game days, and 35 and 20 minutes before each kickoff (section 17). Any snapshot that sends an alert also rebuilds that league's board NEW, any day of the week, so the Bet sheet is never older than the push you just got.

The phone alerts

After every snapshot, any listed side that is new, or whose status changes (Live → Partly gone → Missed), is pushed to your phone through the ntfy app and posted to the "opener-alerts" issue on GitHub, which mentions you so the GitHub app notifies you too. A push reads like this:

NFL DAL -7.5 (ARI @ DAL): model -9.2, opened -6.5, now -7.5 (moved 1 toward); 1.8 left. Partly gone. 6-pt tease to -1.5 (72%, Wong)

Setup (done once): install ntfy, subscribe to a private topic name, and save that name (just the name, not the ntfy.sh address) as the repository secret NTFY_TOPIC. The first push after setup lists every current gap; after that you only hear about changes.

What to do with a push

New, Live: this is the moment the edge exists. Find the best price on the side (the sheet's Where column, or your books) and bet it.

Live → Partly gone: the market is moving your way. If you already have it, good: that is closing line value. If you don't, the straight bet is worth much less than its record says. Look at the teaser leg.

→ Missed: the line has reached the model. No straight bet. If the teaser leg clears 73.9% (often a Wong leg), it can go into a two-team teaser with another leg that clears.

College volume can be high: on November 11 the college board had 19 live sides, 11 partly gone and 9 missed in its buckets, against 4 live and 1 partly gone on the NFL board. Treat a very large college gap (the model at 58.3 on an Auburn–Mississippi State total that opened 51.5 and is now 50) as a reason to look, not a reason to bet.

The Best bets alert NEW

Twenty minutes before each kickoff window, after the last pre-kickoff rebuild, the board sends that window's Best bets card to your phone (the same ntfy topic) and as a comment on the "best-bets" issue on GitHub, which emails you. Tapping the push opens the Best bets page on that league's board. It lists the card exactly as the Best Bets record will grade it, but at DraftKings and FanDuel only: each bet is shown at the better of the two that still qualifies under its own rule (a price bet must still beat Pinnacle's no-vig number; anything else must still be +EV). When neither book qualifies, the bet is listed as NOT A BET with the best price at the two books and the book the card used. A window with nothing on the card sends nothing.

5. The Games page, part three: the boost calculator, teasers and alternate lines

Price a boost or a promo

Pick a side, enter the boosted odds the book is offering (or the base odds and a profit-boost percentage), choose a book, and the calculator prices it against Pinnacle's no-vig chance and against the model. A boost that is +EV against the sharp number is the best bet a recreational bettor ever gets. Boosts are capped by the book, so size to the cap, not to the Kelly number.

Teasers and alternate lines

The teaser table on November 11: BUF −1.5 (76.5%) and WAS +8.5 (75.7%) are Wong legs that clear 73.9%; NE +8.5 is a Wong leg that does not (72.9%).

Choose 6 or 7 points and your book's two-leg price, and every leg is listed with its chance and whether it clears. "Market curve" prices legs with no model, which is the honest default. Closing line value: the number to watch first NEW

The Closing line value section on November 11: 54 NFL bets with a closing value, averaging +1.8% ± 1.7%; each kind's stake rule ("Watching 26 of 50"), and each kind's running average, bet by bet.

CLV is the EV of the price you took, measured at the closing no-vig chance. It's the one short-run proof that bets had edge: a bettor who beats the close consistently wins in the long run, whatever this week's results say. Fifty bets won't separate skill from luck on wins and losses; they will on CLV. Price-tier bets should show positive CLV almost every time. Opener bets should show it whenever the line moved toward them, which is the whole idea.

The section has its own tiles (bets with a closing value, the average with its standard error, how often the close was beaten), a table by kind and by market, and a chart of each kind's running average. The Stake rule column says where each kind stands under the closing-line-value guard (section 2): "Watching (n of 50)" until it has 50 graded bets, then staked, or muted if it is losing to the close by more than two standard errors. The close is Pinnacle's last quote before kickoff; where Pinnacle never quoted a game, as for most college games, DraftKings' and then FanDuel's closing no-vig number is used.

How I read it on November 11 Still early. Across 54 NFL bets the board has beaten the close by 1.8% ± 1.7%: encouraging, not proven. Opener bets are 10-6 and average +2.6%, but only 5 of 16 beat the close, so that average rests on a few big moves. On the college board, where this table was empty until November 11, it reads −0.2% ± 0.8% on 151 bets, with the price tier at −0.1% on 92: there the claimed edge has not shown up, and the price tier's stake rule already reads "Close to the line". How it worksThe method in plain words, and why "chance of cashing" is not the goal.

Reading a parlay card

ChanceThe chance every leg wins. A pushed leg drops out, as the books settle it. Legs in different games multiply; legs in the same game move together, priced from correlations fitted on past games (a quarterback's yards with his receivers', a side with the total, a player's yards with his team covering, a quarterback's touchdown passes with a teammate's touchdown).
Price / BoostedThe parlay's price at the book, and after the boost.
ValueExpected return per dollar at the book's price (after the boost). Negative value is the book's edge.
NeedsThe price the parlay must pay to break even. If the app quotes more, it is a good bet; less, it is not.
FairThe price with no edge either way.
StakeQuarter Kelly on a 100-unit bankroll ($1,000 at $10 a unit), in half units, at most 2 units and never more than the boost's maximum wager. Nothing is staked on a negative-value parlay.
Each legIts chance, its price at the book, and its own value. Prop legs also show the player's share of his team's targets or carries this game, his usual share when the two differ by 2 points or more, and "teammates out" when his team has players ruled out.

The Record tab on November 11: NFL legs 51-32 for the season (61.5% against the 55.5% the tool said), best 2-leg parlays 8-24, best 3-leg 8-26; college legs 399-376 (51.5% against 49.2% said).

Where the chances come from

  • Game lines (spreads, totals, moneylines and moved lines): the board's market-adjusted projection read off the key-number curve, shrunk to the market by the haircut. On both boards that means the market's own number: the legs carry no model edge the results have not earned.
  • NFL props: DraftKings' and FanDuel's no-vig number, nudged toward the model by at most 7.5 points (3 before November 4). Value is scored on the books' number alone, so a prop shows value only when its price earns it: one book better than the other, a rung priced better than its neighbours, or a boost.
  • College props: where DraftKings or FanDuel post the prop, the books' own chance at their real prices; two-sided main lines (passing yards and TDs, rushing and receiving yards) nudged toward the model by at most 3 points (provisional from November 13, rechecked in January), value still scored on the books' number. Lines the books do not post keep the model's chance, tagged "not market-tested", with widths fitted on college games since November 4; leave the "Include college props the books don't post" box unticked unless you know why you want them.
How a professional uses this page

A parlay without a boost is almost always negative value: every leg pays the book's margin. The page earns its keep with boosts. When a book offers one, enter it in the Boost finder, set the book, the maximum wager and the game if the boost names one, and take the top card at the book that offered the boost. Check every leg's price in the app first, and skip the parlay if the app quotes less than "Needs".

Don't rank by chance of cashing. Heavy favourites parlayed together lose more than they win: in 2025, alternate rungs priced −400 to −1000 cleared less often than their prices said. The boosted 5-leg card above, at +37.7% value, still loses about 19 times in 20. Stake it small and expect long losing runs.

Read the Record tab like the Best Bets record: is the hit rate near the chance the tool said, and is the return near what it claimed? On November 11 the NFL legs were 51-32 (61.5% against 55.5% said) and the college legs 399-376 (51.5% against 49.2% said); the best NFL 2-leg parlays were 8-24 (25% against 29% said). Two weeks of NFL legs still say little; the college legs are tracking what the tool claimed.

Check the injury report (it changes the model's number, shown in red) and the Bet sheet after each morning's update. NFL quarterback changes now reach the board automatically by noon, Monday to Saturday, through the starter check. A bet that leaves the sheet is marked withdrawn. That is the board changing its mind, and you should know why before you do anything about it.

Friday and Saturday (college), Saturday and Sunday (NFL)

  • Download the Best bets PDF the morning of the games. That is the card. Twenty minutes before each kickoff window, the Best bets alert gives you the final card at DraftKings and FanDuel: bet what it lists, skip what it marks NOT A BET.
  • If a book offers a profit boost, run it through the Parlays page's Boost finder and take the top card at that book, after checking each price in the app.
  • Take price bets only at the price shown or better. With hourly game-day snapshots the board's prices are about an hour old; if a row is greyed with "Check the price", it is older than three hours.
  • Bet wind unders as soon as the wind is in the forecast, at the half stake shown.
  • For Missed sides, look at the teaser table for legs that clear 73.9%, Wong legs first.
  • Check the weekly cap line. If it bit, your stakes are already scaled.

What to ignore

  • The NFL model's own edges and every game's lean (Model leans). Information, not bets.
  • College model edges. They are on the sheet so they are recorded, but they carry no stake.
  • Big EV numbers on model edges without reading the stake column.
  • Rain and snow tags. Information until the forecasts have been graded.
  • Unboosted parlays, and parlays ranked by chance of cashing.
  • A week's win-loss record. Watch CLV.
  • Prop flags (there are none) and prop "leans".

19. Reviewing your bets: what a loss can and cannot teach you

A loss on a bet that beat the closing line was a good bet that lost. A win on a bet that the line moved against was a bad bet that won. Review in this order:

  1. Did I get the price? Compare your price to the board's and to the close. CLV is the grade, and the Closing line value section shows it by kind of bet.
  2. Was it on the card at kickoff? The Best Bets record and the Live record's kept/withdrawn split tell you whether to trust the first word or the last.
  3. Was the reason right? On the game page, did the model's number rest on news that turned out wrong (a quarterback who didn't start)?
  4. Only then, the result. Spread bets near 50% will lose five in a row about one week in thirty-two by chance alone.

20. Dictionary

Anchor. The book a game's projection is tied to: Pinnacle when it quotes the game, else the consensus.

Best bets alert. The final card for a kickoff window at DraftKings and FanDuel only, pushed to your phone and emailed 20 minutes before kickoff (section 4).

Best Bets record. The record of only the bets on the Best bets card as it stood at each kickoff (section 14).

Boost finder. The Parlays page tool that finds the best parlays for a profit boost (section 16).

Break-even. The win rate a price needs to be worth nothing: 52.4% at −110.

Bucket. A kind of bet with its own record: price, opener, wind, model.

Bucket stake. A stake sized from the bucket's record instead of the model's, at the book with the best price against Pinnacle; "(bucket)" on the sheet.

Check the price. The verdict on a price bet whose quote is more than three hours old.

CLV (closing line value). The EV of the price you took at the closing no-vig chance. Positive means you beat the close.

CLV guard. The rule that takes the stake off a kind of bet with 50+ graded bets whose closing line value is below zero by more than two standard errors; its bets stay listed, marked muted.

Close win rate. How the opener bucket's sides did graded at the closing line: 51.9% for NFL spreads 2+, below break-even.

Defense against each position. A defense's record against wide receivers, tight ends and backs, against what those players were projected for, applied to the player projections as a small factor.

EV. Expected value per dollar staked.

Haircut. The share of the model's claimed edge that has shown up as profit in the last eight seasons; stakes are sized from it. NFL 11% ± 29% on November 11 (model edges kept off the card); college measured 52% but capped at 0 since November 1.

Keep rule. Since November 11, 2026: a model change is kept if it is better on every measure its test reports, on the tuning seasons and the holdout, the holdout by at least one standard error; it is then provisional until rechecked on a season it has not seen. (November 4 to 10: any gain, however small.)

Record-adjusted EV. The EV at the chance the stakes are sized from: the model's edge counted only at the share that has shown up in graded bets. It leads wherever a model EV is shown, with the model's own claim under it.

Kept / withdrawn. Whether a ledger bet was still on the sheet at kickoff, or came off before.

Kickoff refresh. The line snapshot and board rebuild 35 and 20 minutes before each kickoff window.

Kelly. The stake that maximizes long-run growth for a given edge; the board uses a quarter of it.

Line movement statuses. Live (the gap still clears the threshold, or the line has moved no more than half a point), Partly gone (the line has come part of the way), Missed (the line has reached the model).

Leg finder. The Parlays page tool that ranks parlays of 2 to 10 legs by value or by chance of cashing (section 16).

Market-adjusted projection. The market's line moved part of the way toward the model (28% on NFL spreads, 5% on totals, 17% for early lines). Every bet is priced from it.

Missed. An opener bet the market has already moved to, or a wind under whose total has already dropped 1.5 points.

Model leans. The document of the model's opinions that are not bets (section 7).

Needs. On a parlay card, the price it must pay to break even.

No-vig. A price with the book's margin removed: the market's estimate of the true chance.

ntfy. The phone app that receives the line-movement pushes; the topic name is the repository secret NTFY_TOPIC.

Opener. The first line posted for a game. The board uses the data's opener (nfelo for the NFL, cfbfastR for college), the number the bucket was backtested on, then Pinnacle's first stored quote, then the first line the board saw.

Nudge. How far an NFL prop leg's chance is moved from the books' no-vig number toward the model on the Parlays page: at most 7.5 points. Value is still scored on the books' number.

Pinnacle. The sharp book every price is judged against.

Post at. The highest Kalshi contract price at which a resting (maker) order is still +EV against the chance the stakes are sized from.

Price tier. A retail price that beats Pinnacle's no-vig number by 1% or more, with no model.

Rain / snow tag. A game forecast to get 2 mm or more of rain, or any snow; shown, not bet.

Raw model. The model's projection before it is weighed against the market.

Teaser. A parlay with every line moved in your favor; two legs of 6 at −120 need each leg above 73.9%.

Vig. The book's margin.

Week 18 at the market. The model's Week 18 spreads and moneylines are not bet (opener and model tiers, alerts); price bets and totals still count.

Weekly cap. 10% of bankroll on one week's bets together; above it every stake that week shrinks by the same factor.

Wind under. An outdoor NFL under in 10–19 mph of forecast wind, staked at half size until graded on forecasts.

Wong teaser. A 6-point teaser leg that crosses both 3 and 7: a favorite of −7.5 to −8.5 or an underdog of +1.5 to +2.5. Price them, do not bet them by rule.
