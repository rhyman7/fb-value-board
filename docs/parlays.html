Parlay tools

Every leg here is priced the same way the boards price bets: game lines off the market-adjusted projection, NFL props from the books' no-vig number moved toward the model, college props from the model alone.

Your slip

What the tools would have suggested, logged before kickoff at the price they had then and graded afterwards, one unit on each (nflmodel/parlaylog.py). Legs: every leg with a DraftKings or FanDuel price worth at least break-even, 35-95% to win. Best 2-leg and Best 3-leg: the Leg finder's top value parlay from different games, at each book. A pushed leg or a player who did not play drops out, as the books settle it. Claimed is the average value the tool showed; a record that keeps up with it is the test.

What the numbers mean

  • Chance is the chance every leg wins (a pushed leg drops out of the parlay, as the books settle it). Legs in different games are independent, so their chances multiply. Legs in the same game move together; their joint chance comes from correlations fitted on past games (a quarterback's yards with his receivers', a side with the total, a player's yards with his team covering), and the card says so.
  • Value is the expected return per dollar at the book's price: +8% means $10 returns $10.80 on average over many bets. Negative value is the book's edge. A boost pays extra on the profit and is often the only thing that makes a parlay worth betting.
  • Needs is the price the parlay has to pay to break even. If the app quotes more than that, it is a good bet; less, it is not.
  • Stake is quarter Kelly on a 100-unit bankroll ($1,000 at $10 a unit), in half units, capped at 2 units and at the boost's maximum wager. Nothing is staked on a negative-value parlay.

Where the chances come from

  • Spreads, totals, moneylines and moved lines: the board's market-adjusted projection, read off the key-number curve, so moving a spread through 3 or 7 costs what those numbers are worth.
  • NFL props: DraftKings and FanDuel have been the better forecasters (PROPS.md), so the chance shown is the books' no-vig number nudged toward the model (at most 3 points). Value is scored on the books' number alone: in 2025, props the nudge made look +EV lost about 4% to the closing line, so a prop only shows value when its price earns it (one book better than the other, a rung priced better than its neighbours, or a boost). A prop line the books do not post carries the correction from the nearest line they do price, and has no price.
  • College props: the model's own distribution only. They have never been compared with sportsbook prices, so they are tagged not market-tested and priced at fair odds.

Why "chance of cashing" is not the goal

Heavy favourites parlayed together lose more than they win. In 2025, DraftKings and FanDuel alternate rungs priced −400 to −1000 cleared 79.5% of the time against the 85.3% their prices implied: about −7% a leg, −13% for two and −19% for three. Ranking by chance shows those parlays honestly, with their value next to them; ranking by value finds the legs closest to a fair price, which is what makes a boost pay.

Legs with no book price are scored at an estimated price (the fair price with a 5% hold on game lines and 10% on props) so they can be ranked; the card marks them and says what price to ask for.
